Before the World Blows: Erykah Badu & The Alchemist's Hypnotic Slow Burn

Back when I wrote about Baduizm, I said Erykah Badu hypnotized me at 16 and I've been under ever since. So when she linked up with The Alchemist for Before the World Blows, her first album in over 15 years, I already knew what I was hoping for. I wanted that spell again.

For the most part, that's exactly what this tape delivers. It's hazy, unhurried and built on loops that sink into you the longer you sit with them. Alchemist lays down beats that breathe, and Badu floats over them like she has all the time in the world. But after a few listens and scoring ten of the tracks one by one, a clear pattern came through: this album is at its best when it grooves, and it loses me when it stalls.

A hypnotic way in

The opener “Echos” sets the tone straight away. It's very chilled and hypnotic, leaning on repetitive phrasing that pulls you deeper with every loop. It's one of my highest-rated joints on the project, and it tells you exactly what kind of ride you're on.

That mood runs right through the tape. “Crossfade” keeps things chilled and easy, the kind of record you leave on without thinking about it. Later, “Love Me Not” slows everything right back down. It's very chill, slow and slightly psychedelic, Badu and Alchemist at their most dreamlike. This is the album's home base, and it's a comfortable place to be.

Where the groove takes over

Where the album really comes alive, though, is when the bass gets involved. “Which Doctor” is my favourite on the whole project: a bit funky, slightly groovy, with a bassline that carries the entire track. It's the one I keep running back.

“Valentine,” featuring DRAM, is pure vibes, warm and easy without overcomplicating anything. Then “Next 2 U” slowly builds, getting more upbeat and funkier as it goes while holding on to that vibey feel. “To the Moon” pulls off the same trick: upbeat, yet slow. Put these side by side and you see the formula that works best here. Keep the haze, but give it some movement. Every time Alchemist lets the groove lead, the album hits its peak.

The few missteps

Not everything lands. Right after the high of “Echos,” “I Just Play a Part” felt underwhelming, and it's easily the lowest point for me. “Abracadabra” is an interlude that moves you along but doesn't give you much to come back to, and “Breezy” is solid without ever really grabbing me.

What these have in common is that they don't have the pull of the stronger tracks. On an album this laid-back, a track needs either that hypnotic loop or that groove to hold your attention. When it has neither, the calm turns into drift.

Final verdict: 7.4/10

Averaged out, the ten tracks I scored land at 7.4, but that number undersells the experience. Six of those ten scored 8 or higher, and at its best, with “Which Doctor,” “Echos,” “To the Moon” and “Valentine,” this is Badu as hypnotic as she's ever been, with Alchemist giving her the perfect bed to float on. Only a couple of moments really pull the score down.

It's not Baduizm, and it doesn't need to be. Before the World Blows is a slow burn that rewards patience, so put it on, let the loops do their thing, and wait for that bass to kick in.
